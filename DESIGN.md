# Tesoro Design

**Direction: Cut, lit.** The game is the star; the platform is quiet, neutral chrome. Tesoro's signature is its *shape* — the emerald cut — and emerald appears only where Gemas move. The mockups are **style references** (look, components, hierarchy), not page layouts: they are vertical images and tuck everything together to fit. Layout and density follow the section "Layout and density" below. Reference 0 was the approved Lobby mockup (the Lobby is gone; Play is home now, see "Below 1024px" and "Play is home") `~/Work/gemstone-labs/tesoro/art-source/ui-redesign/lobby-desktop/take-1.png` (workspace, outside the repo) (1920×1200); structure follows Skillz (`attach/10-ref-skillz-desktop.jpg`), hero drama follows `attach/11-ref-chumba-desktop.jpg`. Original study: `~/Work/gemstone-labs/tesoro/docs/design/emerald-directions-study.html` (the "Cut" row).

Tesoro is **phone first** (founder, Oct 23): phones lead each layout, and desktop shows the same screens widened, keeping its sidebar. A phone's first screen (390×844, no scrolling) shows the screen's main thing: Play shows Continue, Live wins and the games, the Leaderboard the Pool and the ranks, a game its whole board and actions. Mockups set the look; build them with real scrolling and spacing, not one squeezed frame. Phones have no page titles on Leaderboard, Matches and Vault and no site footer; desktop keeps both.

## Principles

- **Chrome stays out of the way.** Near-black neutral shell; game art supplies the colour. No atmosphere, glow, texture or ornament on platform surfaces.
- **Light from above.** Like a cut stone, every cut surface catches light on its top edge and chamfers and fades downward: outlines are vertical gradients (`--edge-1`, `--edge-2`, `--edge-em`) and raised fills are a touch brighter at the top (`--surface`, `--surface-2`). This is the only depth device: no drop shadows on chrome (`clip-path` clips them anyway), no glass.
- **The cut is the brand.** Chamfered (octagonal) corners on every card, chip, button, search box, balance pill, arrow button, avatar and tile — never rounded rectangles or pills. One corner size per component class, and the 1px outline follows the chamfer.
- **Emerald means Gemas move.** Emerald and faceted light (hard-stop gradients) are reserved for the balance pill, the chosen entry, redeemable amounts and money actions (Play now, Enter, Offer, Redeem). If it's emerald, Gemas move. Navigation, chips, arrows and Docs/Help are neutral.
- **Gold means the Leaderboard Pool.** Gold (`--gold-facet`, `--edge-gold`) belongs to the Leaderboard Pool, nothing else: the Pool chest in the top bar (its gold frame also holds your Season points, which stay steel), the Leaderboard Pool band (gold edge and tint) and the Pool figures there (`.pool-fig`), all live via `usePool`. The top-bar Pool chip and the Leaderboard use the same Astra stone chest (`POOL-CHEST-BRIEF.md`, take 3), as `art.poolChestCut` (cut out on transparency, `pool-chest-cut.py` beside the source PNG). The top-bar image is 28px on phones and 40px on desktop, with a bounce/glint when the Pool grows. The flat code-drawn `Chest` remains available for other icon uses. The Leaderboard hero is a banner: the Pool figure huge on the left, and on the right the cut-out chest over slowly turning gold rays (on phones the chest spans the card above the figure). It is never blended, so the chest stays solid in front of the rays. While the season before is still paying out, a gold tab sits on the hero's top edge ("Season N is paying out"; on phones it spans that edge between the cut corners, like a lid). Never on the Gema mark, never on a money action.
- **Plain words, no plumbing.** Players are not technical. UI copy never names the infrastructure: no devnet, rollup, delegate, TEE, VRF, oracle, keeper, commit, base layer, program, LayerZero, EVM, Seed or instruction names (`create_match`). Say what the Player experiences: "Loading the Match…", "Creating your session" (any move into the private rollup), "Dealing your pieces", "Saving the result", "Paying out the Pot", "live price", "crossing chains", "cross-chain fee", "ETH & HYPE address". A wait the Player sits through is one `Busy` line (spinner + text), never a list of technical steps. Errors go through `plainError` (`lib/errors.ts`): the program's own message, or a plain retry line, never a signature or a log. Transaction ids stay available, collapsed, as **Receipts** with plain names (`components/match/Receipts.tsx`). The disclosure line is "Test mode · Gemas come from simulated yield and have no real value." Chain names (Solana, Ethereum, HyperEVM) and trading words in Trading Duel (long, short, leverage, spread) are fine.
- **Plain money words (founder, Oct 19).** The money terms read as plain words, not DeFi: **Lock** (code: Position; "SOL Lock 1", "New Lock"), **Locked amount** (code: Principal; "your SOL" in sentences), **Left to pay back** (code: Debt; "Paid back" at zero), **Length** (code: Horizon), **Earned** (code: Accrued Yield), **test SOL** (code: simSOL). The action that makes a Lock is **Get Gemas**. Kept: Advance, Unlock Estimate, Redeem, Pending, Extend. Source and Staking Rate are never shown. CONTEXT.md names both words for each term; `app/src/plain-words.test.ts` fails if Player-facing text uses an old one.
- **Money is plain, big and honest.** Every balance separates *redeemable now* from *pending*, and pending shows its Sources (one row per Source: asset + Position). On the Vault Gemas card, both views explain simulated yield and the test clock only in the ⓘ tooltip; do not repeat the line under the card (founder, round 9). Other amount surfaces keep the simulated-yield label: every Position card, the Redeem and Return sheets, the ETH Opening receipt and a won Match's pending line (`SimulatedYieldLabel`).
- **Green is not "success".** Because the accent is green, confirmations and wins always carry a second signal (icon or words), never colour alone.
- **Games dominate.** Key art is the only saturated colour on a page. Coming-soon games are dimmed and not clickable.

### The cut

```css
.cut { --c: var(--cut-card); --t: 1px; position: relative; clip-path: polygon(/* octagon by --c */); }
.cut::after { /* evenodd ring: the outline follows the chamfer */ background: var(--edge, var(--line)); }
```

`clip-path` clips real borders, so cut shapes draw their outline as an `::after` evenodd ring coloured by `--edge` (never `border`). Neutral cards use `--edge-1`, controls `--edge-2`, the balance pill and money panels `--edge-em`; money buttons and the chosen entry carry a crown highlight (a white-to-clear edge) plus an inset pavilion shade, and press down 1px. Inputs and selects cannot have pseudo-elements, so each sits in a cut frame (`<span class="cut cut-chip ctl">`) whose `::after` is its edge (`--line-2`) and, focused, its ring; the control itself is transparent and borderless. (Corner gradients on the control broke up on phones.) Classes: `cut-frame` (heroes, sheets), `cut-card` (cards, nav items, chips-as-buttons, tiles), `cut-btn` (buttons, avatars), `cut-chip` (small chips, entry buttons, ticker). Focus never uses `outline` on a cut shape (the clip turns it into a broken rectangle): the edge itself becomes the ring, 2px solid `--fg`, following the chamfers. Light fills (money buttons, the chosen entry, the white Hold'em action, the active Vault tab) take an ink ring set 3px inside instead. A cut wrapper around a bare input, select or summary takes the ring for it; a cut ancestor further up never lights. Dropdowns are `Select` (components/ui/Select.tsx), never a native `<select>`: the browser's own option list ignores our surfaces (it showed white with unreadable text), so the list is a raised cut card (`--raised-2`, `--edge-2`) in the top layer, options in `--fg-2`, the chosen one bold with a check, and it opens above the button when there is no room below. A test fails on any native `<select>`. Two-way (or n-way) view switches, such as Backing's Requests / My Backings, are `Segmented` (components/ui/inputs.tsx, Storybook Components/ui/Segmented): one cut rail with a lit steel piece on the chosen option, a radio group with 44px options, never emerald and never separate buttons.

## Type

- **Display**: Barlow Condensed 800, all caps, for section headers (GAMES), page titles (`.h-section`, `.page-title`) and **game titles wherever a Game is presented as a poster**: the Play tiles, the game page header and a `Row` with a Game and the result word on an Account's Match banner (`MatchBanner`). Also playing-card ranks and leaderboard ranks. Plain rows keep Schibsted.
- **UI text and figures**: Schibsted Grotesk (weights 400/700/800/900), including hero titles, buttons and every amount. `tabular-nums` on amounts in lists and tables (`.tnum`); big display figures (balance pill, sidebar panels, entry amounts) use proportional figures.
- **Verifiable data**: IBM Plex Mono only: chain chips, hashes, Seeds, timers, the footer's bottom strip, the simulated-yield label.

Heavy Schibsted (800–900) gets `word-spacing: .14em` (Barlow does not need it).

**Type scale** (`globals.css`): `--fs-9`, `--fs-10`, `--fs-11`, `--fs-12`, `--fs-13`, `--fs-14`, `--fs-15`, `--fs-16`, `--fs-17`, `--fs-18`, `--fs-20`, `--fs-22`, `--fs-24` (the number is the px size). Text sizes up to 24px use a token, never a half-pixel or one-off value (`app/scripts/check-font-sizes.mjs` warns); larger display sizes and `clamp()` stay raw. The landing and the waitlist keep their own tuned sizes. Play screens (Practice and Match headers on all three Games) title the Game in the display face, like every other page.

## Logo

The Tesoro logo is the **Facet T**: an emerald-cut octagon in four flat facets (logo v2, Oct 10; replaces the Sep 30 T-gem). The light table facet across the top and the mid stem facet between two darker side facets form the T, so the letter is part of the cut. Facets meet by tone only: no outline channels, no gradients. The wordmark is **TESORO in chamfered caps**: drawn, not typeset, every outer corner cut at 45° and octagon O's, the same cut as the app's cards and buttons. Everything is generated from geometry by `app/scripts/build-brand.mjs` into `app/public/brand/` (marks, wordmark, lockups) and `src/components/brand/paths.ts`; the React components are `TesoroMark` (`variant="full|mono"`, `tone="dark|light"`) and `TesoroLogo` in `components/brand`. Brief, Astra's takes and review: `~/Work/gemstone-labs/tesoro/art-source/brand-v2/`; marketing copies in `brand-v2/export/`.

- **Variants.** `full` (four facets; holds down to 16 px, so there is no separate small mark), `mono` (the T alone in currentColor: the side facets drop away). Lockups: horizontal (sidebar, landing nav, footer) and stacked, each in a dark-background version (off-white #F5F3EE wordmark) and a light-background version (#0C0E10 wordmark, deeper emerald facets). Never use one on the other's background.
- **Proportions.** Horizontal lockup: wordmark cap height 0.46 of the mark, gap 0.28. Stacked: wordmark 1.6 x the mark's width, gap 0.2.
- **Clear space** = the stem's width (about a quarter of the mark) on every side. **Minimum sizes:** mark 16 px, horizontal lockup 96 px wide, stacked lockup 72 px wide.
- **Never recolour**, restyle, stretch, rotate, add effects or outlines, or retype the wordmark.
- **Facet T = brand; faceted Gema gem = currency.** Never use the Facet T for currency (balance pill, entry chips, amounts) and never use the plain gem as the logo. The Hold'em card back carries the Facet T in steel, never emerald.
- **Icons and social cards are rendered from the vector** by `build-brand.mjs`: `favicon.ico` (16/32/48), `icon.png` (192, transparent), `apple-icon.png` and the `manifest.ts` 192/512 icons (the mark at 60% on the shell colour with a soft light from the upper left), the maskable 512 (mark in the safe zone on #0C0E10), and `opengraph-image.png` / `twitter-image.png` (1200×630: the Block Burst art on the right, lockup and headline set from vectors on the left).
- Contrast: the wordmark is 17.4:1 on both backgrounds. On paper the light-version table facet (#5FD9AE) is decoration only; the silhouette is carried by the deep side facets.

## Money art: the facet crown

The Vault balance panel (`.facet-panel`) is itself cut as an emerald step cut: `<FacetCrown>` (components/ui) draws three thin bevels that follow the panel's chamfers (30px deep, 13px under 600px wide so they stay inside the padding), lit from above with the top facets brightest, and two faint hard-stop light bands across the table the figures sit on. It is measured to the panel, so the facet angles never stretch. Money surfaces only. (Picked from five prototypes on Oct 11; the others are on branch `proto/facet-crown-11-10-2026`.)

The `BalanceCard` (`.balance`) is a banknote: the whole card is the money fill (crown rim on top, two faint facet light bands, dark pavilion band at the base, a hard-stop facet line across it), with the title and "≈ $" value on top, the figure and Gema mark in ink (glint kept), and "Value at issuance" beside the simulated-yield label under a rule. Proportional figures, up to 64px with the card's width. (Picked from four prototypes on Oct 11; the others are on branch `proto/balance-card-11-10-2026`.)

## The Gema mark

A small emerald-cut stone (octagon with table facet and facet lines, emerald gradient) — never a flat ◆ glyph, never gold. It precedes Gema amounts of consequence (balance pill, entry chips); it is **not** the logo (see Logo). A brief glint animation is allowed on the balance only, and is off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. On a money fill (money button, chosen entry) the full-colour mark blends in, so it is drawn in **ink**: body in the text colour (`--em-ink`), table and facet lines in `--gem-ink-line`. Disabled money buttons keep the full-colour mark.

## Implementation decisions

- `--fg-3` (40%) fails AA for text: decoration only; all text uses `--fg` or `--fg-2`.
- Only Gema actions (Play now, Enter, Offer, Redeem, Take an Advance, sliders that set Gema amounts) and the balance are emerald.
- Money buttons use `--em-ink` for text and the inset focus ring.
- Match results always pair an icon with words: ✓ Won · ✕ Lost · − Draw / Refunded (Play Again tiles too).
- Simulated Unlock Estimates show an absolute time (the yield clock runs 1 month = 10 min); "–" until one exists.
- Players show by **one rule everywhere**: Profile name, else the `.sol` name, else the `.eth` name, else the short key. A `.sol` / `.eth` name carries a small verified mark (an octagon check in `--fg-2`, with "verified .sol name" for screen readers); a Profile name never does, because anyone may pick any name. The picture is the Profile's pick (one of twelve animal heads cut from gemstones, or the picture of the attached `.sol` / `.eth` record) and otherwise the stable animal derived from the Account key (`avatarFor`). Avatars never show people, and never a green stone or a loose gem (emerald and the gem mark belong to Gemas). A picture from a name record is a remote image: shown as is, without a referrer, and replaced by the derived animal when it fails to load. One component draws it all: `PlayerName` / `PlayerAvatar` / `usePlayerLabel` (`components/account/Player.tsx`) over the identity store (`lib/identity/`), never `shortKey` or `avatarFor` on a Player directly. Names load after the page and never block it: the short key shows until they arrive.
- **Edit profile** is a sheet (Account menu, and the Edit profile button on your own `/account/<key>`): a live preview, the name field (3 to 20 letters, digits, spaces, dots, dashes, underscores), a grid of the twelve animals plus one tile for the name-service picture when there is one, the connected wallets with their names and a "show this name" choice, and one Save (one transaction; a wallet that is attached approves once). Where the deployed program has no profiles yet the form is read-only and says "Profiles are coming soon" in place of Save.
- Dev only: `?dev=out` previews the signed-out UI and `?dev=demo` a populated fake Account (with `NEXT_PUBLIC_DEV_WALLET=1`).

## Layout and density

The founder's rule (Oct 1): the website must not copy the mockups' density. Mockups are vertical images that tuck everything together; the app scrolls.

- **Pages scroll, without dead bands.** Do not fit everything into one viewport, but do not leave empty rows either (founder, Oct 2: "too much empty space"). Each section has one job, with `--section-gap` 36px (52px from 1024px) between sections and a capped, centred column (max 1320px); 1920px screens get margins, not more stuff.
- **One primary action per screen**, obvious. Secondary information goes behind progressive disclosure: `<details>` toggles ("Details"), hover and tooltips, and sheets or drawers that open on demand (Get Gemas, Post a Request, Make an Offer, Redeem, the Hold'em Hand log).
- **Fewer simultaneous panels**, fewer labels and meta lines, fewer type sizes, more line-height. A number appears once per screen.
- **Global chrome is minimal**: the sidebar is navigation only, the top bar holds the Pool, the balance pill and the Account, the footer (desktop only; phones have none) is links plus the fair-play and simulated-yield strip, no calls to action.
- **Play screens** centre the game (board or table) with a slim bar above; side columns do not exist. Info that is not needed mid-play moves to the result.
- **Mobile (390px)** applies the same simplifications; secondary content goes in sheets; nothing crowds horizontally.

## Waitlist (`/`)

- **Layout.** The form is bottom left, the panel's gems break into game footage that fills the other half. One 360 x 300 tile (`waitlist/texture.ts`): an octagon with a nested outline whose corner facets link at 45 degrees to the facing facets of its diagonal neighbours. Every line joins two shapes: no stubs ending in empty space. The links are cut at the tile edge in the SVG; change `texture.ts`, both `--lp-texture`s, `--wl-texture` and the divider's pattern together.
- **The texture is lit** (`LivingTexture.tsx`, a canvas above the divider). Only the texture's lines and cells take the light, masked to the panel plus the divider's ground and gems, so the light runs right up into the gems. A slow wash drifts top left; a soft light follows the cursor; a band parallel to the divider eases from the far corner up to it every 11 s; short lights climb the links towards the divider at a steady speed (always the line heading most directly at it, never back), fading in and out across the divider's band; while the email field has focus the cells around the form brighten and the lights start there. White light only, never emerald. Under reduced motion or Save-Data: one still lit patch.
- **The split.** The diagonal runs from the top edge at 28% across to the bottom edge at 72%, so the dark panel and the video wall each take half of the wide frame (phone: a flatter line across the middle, top half footage). - **The divider is the panel growing into the footage** (`Divider.tsx`, `dividers/octa.ts`). Small flat octagon gems in exactly the panel's colour and texture (an SVG pattern anchored at the frame origin, so the texture runs on across them), no outlines, shadows, jewel colours or hollow cells. They sit on a 30px grid (20px on a phone) that divides the texture tile and is centred on its octagon centres, so texture lines run along gem rows. A solid, fused band (~76px past the line) is the panel's edge. From it, 6 long fingers (3 on a phone) of connected gems grow outward (deterministic, Eden-like: a seeded reach profile along the diagonal says how far each stretch may grow, only sites linked edge to edge to the band survive, so the tips are ragged and gem-shaped), at most about 260px past the line, plus a few breakaway gems (about 6% of the growth) one empty cell beyond a tip. In the growth zone the gems are not fused: each starts at its full cell where a finger leaves the band and shrinks to 72% at the tips, so the gaps of footage between neighbours open as the fingers grow out, the centres staying on the lattice. The corner diamonds are filled only in the band and where gems still fill their cell. The floor's straight edge is pulled back under the band (`INSET`, `clipBelowInset`). Static; the exact layout only fades in over the first paint, and not at all under reduced motion.
